Psych 101 · Section 12.4

Why We Go Along
With Others

People can take the same action for different reasons. A group, a request, or an authority figure can shape what we decide.

Conformity Compliance Obedience

Same project. Same choice. Different reasons.

A group must choose how to present its project. In each version below, one student prefers making a website but agrees to make a video.

Group influence

Conformity

Changing your behavior or opinion to match a group.

The example

The student prefers making a website. Everyone else votes for a video, so the student agrees to fit in. Nobody directly asks or orders them to change their vote.

Why it fits: The student goes along because of the group’s choice.

Conformity can involve wanting to fit in or believing the group knows better. This example focuses on fitting in.

A direct request

Compliance

Changing your behavior in response to a direct request.

The example

The student prefers making a website. A classmate asks, “Will you support making a video?” The student agrees.

Why it fits: The student agrees in response to a classmate’s direct request.

An authority’s instruction

Obedience

Following an order or instruction from an authority figure.

The example

The student prefers making a website. The teacher instructs the student to make a video, and the student agrees to follow the instruction.

Why it fits: The student follows an instruction from the teacher, an authority figure.

The key difference

What leads to the “yes”?

Conformity
Influence from the group.
Compliance
A direct request from someone.
Obedience
An order from an authority figure.

In real life, more than one influence can affect a decision.
